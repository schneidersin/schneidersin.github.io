2. The game: free, with ads

Hamster Roll 3D is a racing game: you roll a hamster in a ball round wild courses, answer quiz questions on the way (for school grades 1 to 10), and race computer racers or friends.

It's free. The game costs nothing to download and play. To pay for it, it shows ads from Google AdMob:

  • between races played on the device, at most one ad every 2 races, and never in online or Wi-Fi games;
  • and, only if the player taps a button that says WATCH AN AD, an ad that gives what the button says:
    • DOUBLE SEEDS, on a race's results: the seeds from that race again;
    • +100 SEEDS, in the Store: 100 seeds;
    • WEAR IT FOR 3 RACES, in the Store, on a look that seeds can buy: that look to wear for 3 races (section 4);
    • ONE MORE LIFE, when the player is out of Tumble Tower or a Colosseum game with lives (Knockout, Teams 3 v 3) played alone on the device: back in with one life, once in a match;
    • TRIPLE YOUR GIFT, on the main menu after the day's gift is opened: twice that gift's seeds again.

No ads + all looks (section 3) turns off all of them.

Every ad request asks Google to treat the player as a child, to show only ads suitable for general audiences (rated G), and not to personalise the ads. The Privacy Policy says exactly when ads appear and what Google's ads software collects.

The ads are chosen and shown by Google, not by me. If an ad looks wrong for children, please email me what it showed, so I can try to block it.

What needs a connection. Racing on your own works without the internet. The ads, buying or restoring No ads + all looks, the backup and Google sign-in need the internet, and online games need a Wi-Fi network or an internet connection between the players' devices.

3. No ads + all looks

In Settings > ADS, No ads + all looks can be bought with real money. The button shows its price in your own money.

  • What it does: it turns off every ad in the game. There are no ads between races and no WATCH AN AD buttons. Instead, as a thank-you, the seeds from each race played on the device are doubled automatically, without an ad: the same extra seeds that watching the ad would have given. Online games don't double seeds, with or without No ads + all looks, because they never offer the ad. In the same way the day's gift is tripled without an ad, and ONE MORE LIFE is offered without an ad, once in a match. On a device where Google Play says you own No ads + all looks, the game doesn't even start Google's ads software (the Privacy Policy explains). It also unlocks every look in the Store that seeds can buy, straight away and without spending seeds, including looks added in later updates. Five looks can't be bought with seeds and still have to be earned by playing: the Gold and Rainbow balls, the Crown, the Halo and the Rainbow trail.
  • What it doesn't do: buying it gives no seeds by itself. The extra seeds come only with seeds won in races and with the day's gift, and seeds already spent on looks aren't given back. Looks only change how your ball and hamster look, and seeds, doubled or not, only buy looks in the Store (section 4), so they don't make the hamster faster or help it win. The one more life is the same one that any player can have by choosing to watch an ad.
  • You pay once. It is a one-time purchase, not a subscription.
  • Bought through Google Play. Google Play takes the payment, under Google Play's Terms of Service. I never see card or bank details. Children should ask a grown-up before buying anything. Google Play can be set to ask for a password before every purchase, in the Google Play app's settings.
  • Refunds. In the first 48 hours after buying, you can ask Google Play for a refund yourself: Google Play's help page explains how. After that, or if Google Play says no, email me: I can give refunds through Google Play. I will give your money back if No ads + all looks doesn't work as this page says and I can't put it right, or if the law where you live gives you a refund. If a purchase is refunded or cancelled, the ads and the WATCH AN AD: DOUBLE SEEDS button come back, and the seeds from races are no longer doubled automatically. The looks it unlocked are locked again, and anything you were wearing from them goes back to the usual look. Seeds already earned, and looks earned or bought with seeds, stay yours.
  • Changing your mind (EU and UK). If you live in the European Union or the United Kingdom, the law usually gives you 14 days to change your mind about something you buy online. If, when you buy No ads + all looks, Google Play asks you to agree that it starts straight away and that you then lose this right, the 14 days end once it has started. Otherwise you can change your mind within 14 days: ask Google Play, or email me. Your other rights, such as getting your money back if it doesn't work, always stay.

4. Seeds

Seeds are the game's play money.

  • Earned by playing, from the daily gift, or by choosing to watch an ad: for finishing races, good places, gold rings, quiz questions answered right, hidden stars, medals, trophies and more. After a race played on the device, the player can choose to watch an ad to double that race's seeds. With No ads + all looks (section 3), they are doubled automatically, without an ad. In the Store, a player without No ads + all looks can also choose to watch an ad for 100 seeds, as often as an ad is available. Once a day the main menu has a free gift of 50 seeds; an ad watched by choice triples it to 150, and with No ads + all looks it is tripled without an ad.
  • A look to try: in the Store, a player without No ads + all looks can choose to watch an ad to wear a look that seeds can buy for 3 races (each further ad for the same look adds 3). A race counts when it is finished with the look on. The look isn't bought: after its last race it comes off, and what was worn before goes back on. It is kept on that device only. The five looks that can only be earned are never lent.
  • Spent in the Store (called the Wardrobe before version 1.0.2) on looks for the hamster: balls, hats, furs and trails. They change how the hamster looks, not how it races.
  • No money value. Seeds aren't for sale: nothing in the game sells them for money. No ads + all looks, the only thing for sale, gives no seeds: it doubles the seeds earned from races played on the device and triples the day's gift, so they still come only from playing and from the gift, and it unlocks the looks that seeds buy. Seeds can't be sold, traded, given to another player or cashed out, and I never swap them for money or for anything outside the game.
  • Updates can change them. To keep the game fair and fun, an update may change how many seeds things pay, what an ad gives, what things in the Store cost, and what's in the Store.
  • Keep a backup. Seeds and progress are kept on the device, and in the backup if one is on (section 5). Without a backup I have no copy, so if the game is uninstalled or its data is cleared, I can't bring them back.

5. The backup and accounts

The game works fully without an account: progress is kept on the device. A backup is optional. It keeps a copy of the progress at Google Firebase, which Google runs for me, in case the device is lost or the game is reinstalled.

  • Guest backup: PLAY AS A GUEST (BACKED UP) on the ACCOUNT screen. It has no name and no email address.
  • Google sign-in: Sign in with Google keeps the backup with a Google account instead. On phones and tablets it opens only after a neutral question about the year the player was born shows that the player is surely 13 or older (section 7).
  • Signing out isn't deleting. A guest who taps SIGN OUT can't get back in to that backup, so delete it first if you don't want it kept.
  • Deleting: on the ACCOUNT screen, tap DELETE ACCOUNT and then TAP AGAIN TO DELETE FOR GOOD. This needs an internet connection. It deletes the backup and the account; the progress on the device stays. If the account can't be deleted straight away (for example, an account signed in with Google that hasn't signed in recently), the game still deletes the backup, signs out and says what to do next. You can also ask me by email, as the Privacy Policy explains.
  • Unused backups are deleted automatically 2 years after they were last updated, and unused account records are deleted after 2 years too, as the Privacy Policy explains.
  • Fair use: don't try to get into other players' backups, or to overload or attack the backup service. If an account is used that way, I may switch it off or delete it.

6. Playing with others

One player taps HOST ONLINE and the others JOIN ONLINE. Online games run straight between the players' own devices: there is no game server of mine. So I can't watch online games, see what players say or do in them, or step in while they happen.

The rules

  • Be kind. Don't bully, threaten, tease or upset other players.
  • No rude or personal names. On phones and tablets, the hamster's name is picked from a list. Where a name or message can be typed (in the game on a computer), don't type anything rude or hurtful, and don't type anyone's real name or personal details. You are responsible for what you type.
  • No cheating. Don't change the game or its files, use tools or tricks to get an unfair advantage, or send made-up game messages to spoil a game for others.
  • Don't share personal details. Never tell anyone in a game your real name, school, address or phone number. The HOST ONLINE and JOIN ONLINE screens remind players of this.

Tools that keep games friendly

  • Quick chat only, on phones: phones and tablets can send only 6 fixed phrases (such as GG! and SORRY!) and 4 pictures. They can't type messages, and they don't show messages that others type.
  • Mute: tap the speaker button next to a player's name to hide their quick chat.
  • Kick: the host can tap KICK to take a player out of their game, and the game turns that player's device away if it tries to join that game again.
  • Leave: anyone can leave a game at any time.
  • Chat off: quick chat can be turned off in Settings > Chat. On phones and tablets, that needs a grown-up (section 7).
  • Private games: a hosted game is private at first: friends need its 5-digit room code to join. On phones and tablets, only a grown-up can make a game public.
  • A word filter: names typed on computers are shown with common English swear words starred out. No filter can catch everything.

On a shared or public Wi-Fi network, the people who can see a public game may be strangers.

If someone is unkind: mute them, leave the game, and tell a grown-up. A grown-up can email me about it. Because I can't see online games, I may not be able to find out what happened or who it was.

7. Children and parents

Hamster Roll 3D is made for children and older players: its quizzes cover school grades 1 to 10. A few choices in the game are kept for grown-ups:

  • The age question. On phones and tablets, before Google sign-in, before a hosted game is made public and before chat is changed, the game asks a neutral question: the year the player was born. Google sign-in opens only for a year that makes the player surely 13 or older. Making a game public and changing chat need a year at least 18 years before this one. The game keeps the answer only in its memory while it runs, and never saves it or sends it anywhere. A child can't turn a "no" into a "yes" by typing another year: a new year is only taken after the game is closed and started again. The one exception: if the game has been out of sight for 5 minutes, it asks again before letting anything through, in case someone else is now holding the device, and the new answer can only close things, never open more. The question can't prove anyone's age, so please keep these choices for grown-ups.
  • Purchases: No ads + all looks is bought through Google Play, which can ask for a password before every purchase (section 3).
  • How a child is doing: Settings > PLAYTEST LOG lists the races played on the device and the quiz questions answered wrongly, so you can see where your child gets stuck.
  • The right quizzes: the school grade for the quizzes can be changed in Settings at any time.
  • Information: the game never asks a child for their name, email address, home address, school or phone number. The Privacy Policy explains children's privacy and what parents can see, change and delete.

Parents can email me at blackaurorastar@gmail.com about anything to do with their child and the game.
